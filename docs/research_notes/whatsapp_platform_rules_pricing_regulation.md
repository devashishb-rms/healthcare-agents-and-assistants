# WhatsApp platform access, pricing and regulation for a doctor-replying AI agent (as of 20 October 2026)

Scope note: Every fact below is dated and sourced. "Primary" = Meta/government text read directly (Meta developer docs were read through a rendered browser session on 20 Oct 2026; the Meta Terms, Business Messaging Policy and Help Center pages likewise). Vendor/BSP blogs are marked as secondary. Items that could not be verified are in "Gaps". Research tooling note: WebSearch/WebFetch quota ran out early in this session; most sources were pulled directly (curl / rendered browser), so coverage of press commentary is thinner than coverage of primary documents.

## Key Question 1 — Technical access paths: how can an automated agent legitimately read and reply on a doctor's WhatsApp number?

### Takeaway
There is exactly one Meta-sanctioned way for software to read and send messages programmatically: the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API), reached directly from Meta or through a Business Solution Provider. A personal (WhatsApp Messenger) number cannot be read by a bot at all without violating WhatsApp's terms; the practical path is personal number → WhatsApp Business App (free, keeps chats) → "Coexistence" onboarding to Cloud API (keeps the app usable on the phone, syncs up to 6 months of 1:1 history, mirrors app-sent messages to the API). The On-Premises API is dead (expired 23 Oct 2025). Unofficial routes (whatsapp-web.js, Baileys/whatsmeow-based MCP servers, Android notification-listener auto-responders) all violate WhatsApp's Terms of Service, carry an explicit, documented ban and legal-action risk, and are unsuitable for a clinical workflow.

### Cited Findings

Official paths
- Meta's own statement (undated page, live 20 Oct 2026): "we've built two tools – the WhatsApp Business app and the WhatsApp Business Platform – to help companies manage customer interactions. Our products are not intended for bulk or automated messaging, both of which have always been a violation of our Terms of Service" … "WhatsApp will take legal action against those we determine are engaged in or assisting others in abuse that violates our Terms of Service, such as automated or bulk messaging, or non-personal use" (since 7 Dec 2019), and bans via "machine-learning classifiers" continue. — [WhatsApp Help Center: Unauthorized use of automated or bulk messaging](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5957850900902049)
- On-Premises API sunset (primary): "The final supported version of the On-Premise API client expired on October 23, 2025. On-Premises API can't be used to send messages to WhatsApp users anymore." Timeline: 9 Jan 2024 new features Cloud-only; 1 Jul 2024 new numbers could only register for Cloud API; 23 Oct 2025 v2.63 expired. Cloud API offers "up to 1,000 messages per second (4x the level of On-Premises API)", "99.9% uptime and < 5 seconds p99 latency", SOC2/SOC3, GDPR/LGPD compliance statements, and "local storage options". — [Meta: On-Premises API Sunset](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/on-premises/sunset)
- Non-template ("service") messages can only be sent inside a 24-hour customer-service window that "opens and resets with each user message"; template messages can be sent any time. Service messages "can be powered by a 3rd-party AI solution or people (like customer service representatives)" — i.e., Meta explicitly contemplates third-party AI generating in-window replies. (Page updated 30 Sep 2026.) — [Meta: Pricing on the WhatsApp Business Platform](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Meta also sells its own hosted reply agent, "Meta Business Agent", as a 5th message category since 1 Jul 2026, priced at US$2.00 per 1M tokens (~4–5¢/message). — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)

Coexistence (WhatsApp Business App + Cloud API on the same number)
- 360dialog partner docs (page "last updated 1 month ago" as of 20 Oct 2026; secondary but BSP-operational): "Onboarding a number already registered with the WhatsApp Business App allows you to enable API functionalities without losing chat history and while maintaining access through the mobile app." "Messages will be mirrored between the mobile App and the API, using echoes. Messages sent via the API will appear in the WhatsApp Business App. Messages sent via the App will appear in the Cloud API conversation history." — [360dialog: WhatsApp Coexistence](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/waba-management/whatsapp-coexistence)
- Same source, requirements/limits: "You can sync message history from the WhatsApp Business App into the Cloud API"; "Do not uninstall the WhatsApp Business App - doing so will disconnect the account"; "You must open the WhatsApp Business App at least once every 13 days to keep the account active"; after onboarding "all companion apps will be unlinked" and "WhatsApp for Windows and WhatsApp for WearOS are not supported"; Classic Business Verification and Official Business Account (blue badge) are not supported for coexistence accounts (Meta Verified for Business is); "COEX numbers can't be migrated between WABAs"; "Businesses with newly created Business App accounts are not immediately eligible for API access. Eligibility is determined based on factors such as account tenure and messaging quality"; "this process only works for numbers registered in the App. If the number is connected to the WhatsApp API, this Coexistence Onboarding won't work"; disconnect path is in-app: Settings > Account > Business Platform > Disconnect. — [360dialog: WhatsApp Coexistence](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/waba-management/whatsapp-coexistence)
- Telnyx release note dated 7 Oct 2026: coexistence lets you "Connect a phone number already active in the WhatsApp Business App … without deregistering it from the app"; "The Business App and Cloud API share the phone number but do not share billing or customer service window state"; "Synchronize up to six months of individual chat history during onboarding, or skip it entirely"; messages sent manually from the app "produce a message.echo webhook"; requires "WhatsApp Business App to version 2.24.17 or later". — [Telnyx: WhatsApp Coexistence Now Available](https://telnyx.com/release-notes/whatsapp-coexistence)
- ChakraHQ (BSP blog, originally 18 Mar 2025, updated 2026): "When WhatsApp first launched Coexistence in February 2025, it was a limited beta. Since then, it's expanded to become a globally available feature — including the EU, UK, and Australia"; chat sync "over the past 6 months"; after onboarding, Business App users lose disappearing messages and view-once. — [ChakraHQ coexistence guide](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-coexistence-business-app-register-cloud-api/)
- Contradiction: Seasalt.ai (vendor blog, 2025) says coexistence is "Only available in select countries (e.g., India, Brazil, Mexico, Indonesia, US, Hong Kong, Singapore); not supported in the EU, UK, Australia, Japan, South Africa, Russia, Turkey", and that "disappearing messages, live location, message edit/revoke" are disabled in coexistence mode. — [Seasalt.ai: WhatsApp Coexistence technical architecture](https://seasalt.ai/en/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-technical-architecture-enterprise-use-and-the-future-of-hybrid-messaging); contradicted on geography by [ChakraHQ](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-coexistence-business-app-register-cloud-api/). India is on every list as supported.
- Search-engine summaries of vendor pages (unverified against Meta) put coexistence API throughput at 20 messages/second vs 80 for a standalone API number, and state group chats do not sync. Source for throughput claim: Seasalt.ai (see above); the group-chat non-sync point is consistent with Meta's Groups API page which says groups "are not available for WhatsApp Business app phone numbers" (see KQ7).
- Pricing under coexistence: "All messages sent through the WhatsApp Business App remain free. Messages sent through the WhatsApp Cloud API are subject to WhatsApp Cloud API pricing." — [360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/waba-management/whatsapp-coexistence)

Unofficial paths and their ToS position
- whatsapp-web.js (Puppeteer-driven WhatsApp Web) README disclaimer: "it is not guaranteed you will not be blocked by using this method. WhatsApp does not allow bots or unofficial clients on their platform, so this shouldn't be considered totally safe." Feature table shows it can receive media (images/audio/video/documents) and operate in groups; buttons/lists are deprecated. — [whatsapp-web.js README](https://github.com/pedroslopez/whatsapp-web.js)
- Baileys (WebSocket implementation of the multi-device protocol) README: "The maintainers of Baileys do not in any way condone the use of this application in practices that violate the Terms of Service of WhatsApp… We discourage any stalkerware, bulk or automated messaging usage." and support offers are "not condoning or endorsing businesses to use the library." — [Baileys README](https://github.com/WhiskeySockets/Baileys)
- WhatsApp MCP server (lharries, whatsmeow-based): "connects to your personal WhatsApp account directly via the Whatsapp web multidevice API"; QR-code login; "After approximately 20 days, you will might need to re-authenticate"; stores all messages in a local SQLite DB; author warns it is "subject to the lethal trifecta" (prompt-injection → private data exfiltration). — [whatsapp-mcp README](https://github.com/lharries/whatsapp-mcp)
- Android notification-listener approach: "AutoResponder for WA" (5M+ downloads, 4.4★, updated 9 Oct 2026) "works through Android notification access rather than an official WhatsApp API integration"; "not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by WhatsApp or Meta". It can only see notification text and reply via the notification reply action, i.e., no media access and no reliable history. — [Google Play: AutoResponder for WA](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=tkstudio.autoresponderforwa&hl=en)
- Zylos research note (26 Jan 2026, secondary): after the Jan 2026 general-purpose-assistant ban, "Unofficial libraries like Baileys … remain the only path for personal AI assistants, but carry significant risks including account bans and TOS violations." — [Zylos: WhatsApp API and Automation 2026](https://zylos.ai/research/2026-01-26-whatsapp-api-automation)

### Inferences
- For a clinic bot the only defensible architecture is: doctor's number on the WhatsApp Business App → Coexistence onboarding to Cloud API (direct with Meta via Embedded Signup, or via a BSP) → your agent receives webhooks and sends in-window replies. The doctor keeps replying by hand from the phone app when they want to, and the API sees those replies as echoes.
- "Chat history" survives in two hops: (a) WhatsApp Messenger → WhatsApp Business App migration keeps chats on the device (this is a WhatsApp FAQ-level fact that I could not fetch this session — see Gaps), and (b) Coexistence can sync up to 6 months of 1:1 history into the API. Older history and group chats will not be available to the bot.
- The 13-day "open the app" rule and the "do not uninstall" rule mean the doctor's phone stays a hard dependency; this is unlike a pure API number.
- Any approach that logs into the doctor's personal account with an unofficial client is both a ToS violation and, for patient data, an uncontrolled data store (local SQLite, no audit, no DPA with Meta).

### Gaps
- Meta's own Coexistence developer page could not be located (the URLs under developers.facebook.com tried returned "Page Not Found" or an empty shell); all coexistence specifics above are from BSP documentation (360dialog, Telnyx, ChakraHQ, Seasalt). Country availability conflicts between sources and should be checked against Meta's current list before relying on EU/UK use.
- The WhatsApp FAQ on migrating from WhatsApp Messenger to the WhatsApp Business App (and whether chat history carries over) was not fetched (whatsapp.com pages block plain HTTP fetches and the search budget was exhausted). Treat "history is retained on migration" as widely reported but unverified here.
- Ban-rate statistics for unofficial libraries (e.g., vendor claims that "68% of small Indian businesses were banned within a year") were found only in unsourced vendor pages and are not reliable.

## Key Question 2 — WhatsApp Business Platform pricing (2025–2026): per-message model, India/US rates, free windows, and how a reply bot is billed

### Takeaway
Since 1 July 2025 Meta charges per delivered business→user message by category and recipient market; user→business messages are never charged. The big change for a reply bot: service (free-form, in-window) messages were free from 1 Nov 2024 but are charged again from 1 October 2026 beyond a free tier of 1,000 service messages per business phone number per month; in-window utility templates also stopped being free on 1 Oct 2026. India list rates effective 1 Oct 2026: Marketing ₹0.8631, Utility ₹0.115, Authentication ₹0.115, Service ₹0.115, Auth-international ₹2.4971 (INR card) / US$0.0118, $0.0014, $0.0014, $0.0014, $0.0304 (USD card). North America (US+Canada): Marketing $0.025, Utility/Auth/Service $0.0034.

### Cited Findings
- Model: "Effective July 1, 2025 – Meta charges on a per-message basis… Meta charges by category–market, and only for messages from businesses to users. Meta does not charge for messages from users to businesses. Businesses are only charged when a message is delivered." Rate depends on "the message category" and "the recipient WhatsApp phone number's country calling code ('market')". (Page "Updated: Sep 30, 2026".) — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Five categories as of 1 Jul 2026: Marketing, Utility, Authentication (templates) + Service and Meta Business Agent (non-template). "A message from a business to a user only has 1 of the 5 categories and thus can only incur up to 1 charge." — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Service messages charged again from 1 Oct 2026: "Meta resumed charging, on a per-message basis, for messages that were previously free: Service messages, which became free as of Nov. 1, 2024… Utility messages sent in response to users (thus in an open customer service window), which became free as of July 1, 2025." "For each business phone number, Meta provides 1,000 free service messages per month, which do not roll over but reset monthly. Thus, Meta only charges as of the 1,001st service message for each business phone number." Reset at "12am by Messaging account timezone". "If businesses do not have a payment method… Meta will deliver service messages within the free tier but not deliver them after the free tier has been used." Webhook pricing.type flips from free_customer_service to regular on the 1,001st message. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Exception: "Starting October 1, 2026 - Service messages remain free beyond the monthly free tier only for eligible government and non-profit organizations… This pricing policy will be in effect through December 31, 2027." Eligible cohorts include "Community non-profit organizations, including those in public health, crisis response…". Webhook subtype paid_exempt from 12 Oct 2026. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Free entry point (FEP) window: if a user messages from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad on Android/iOS and the business replies inside the customer-service window, that reply "is not charged and opens a Free Entry Point ('FEP') window… The FEP window may remain open for up to 7 days. While open, Meta does not charge for marketing, utility, authentication or service messages." Meta Business Agent messages are still charged inside FEP. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- 24-hour customer service window: "When a WhatsApp user messages you or calls you, a 24-hour timer called a customer service window starts. If the user messages or calls you again before the timer expires, the timer resets to 24 hours. Businesses can only send non-template messages to users when this window is open." — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- India INR rate card "Cost per message in INR on the WhatsApp Business Platform, effective October 1, 2026": India — Marketing 0.8631; Utility 0.115; Authentication 0.115; Authentication-International 2.4971; Service 0.115; Meta Business Agent $2.00 USD/1M tokens. For comparison on the same INR card: North America Marketing 1.8313 / Utility-Auth-Service 0.2491; United Kingdom 4.6496 / 1.6122; Germany 10.0073 / 4.0322. — Meta "INR list rates" spreadsheet linked from [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing) (download link is session-signed on fbcdn; obtain from the "Rate cards and volume tiers" table).
- USD rate card "effective October 1, 2026": India — Marketing $0.0118; Utility $0.0014; Authentication $0.0014; Auth-International $0.0304; Service $0.0014. North America — Marketing $0.025; Utility/Authentication/Service $0.0034. United Kingdom $0.0635 / $0.022; Germany $0.1365 / $0.055. — Meta "USD list rates" spreadsheet linked from [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Twilio's calculator independently shows Meta's utility/authentication/service fee for the US as "$0.0034 / message", corroborating the North America USD rate. — [Twilio WhatsApp pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/messaging/channels/whatsapp/pricing)
- Meta Business Agent: "One global rate of $2.00 USD per 1 million (M) tokens. One message typically consumes 20,000–25,000 tokens, which translates to approximately 4–5 cents (USD) per message." No free tier, no volume tiers. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Volume tiers apply only to utility and authentication; aggregated at business-portfolio level per market–category; only charged messages count; reset monthly. Illustrative tier discounts −5%/−10%/−15%. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Pricing calendar (since Sep 2025): changes only on 1 Jan / 1 Apr / 1 Jul / 1 Oct, with ≥1 month notice for rate-card updates, 3 months for add-ons, 6 months for model changes. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- India-specific history: 1 Jan 2026 India Marketing rate "Higher"; 1 Apr 2026 India Auth-int'l "Higher"; India's list rates did not change on 1 Oct 2026 (Baat.ai, 7 Oct 2026). Billing localisation: "launched on January 1, 2026 for Solution Providers and directly-integrated businesses whose Sold-To country is India… Eligible customers must ensure all Messaging accounts… are migrated to INR by December 31, 2026… as of January 1, 2027 Meta will no longer deliver the messages of non-INR Messaging accounts of eligible customers." Currency Migration API available since 1 Jun 2026. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing); [Baat.ai India pricing explainer](https://baat.ai/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-india)
- Template categorisation is enforced: "a promotion filed as utility comes back rejected" (Baat.ai, vendor blog). Template messages require prior approval by category (Meta: "see template categorization to learn how template message categorization works"). — [Baat.ai](https://baat.ai/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-india); [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Meta's own cost comparison (10,000 AI-powered replies to Brazilian users): third-party AI "~9 ¢ per message (estimate)" + delivery 0.68¢ = "~$968" vs Meta Business Agent "~$400–$500". — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- GST applies on top for Indian businesses on both Meta fees (when billed in INR) and platform subscriptions (Baat.ai, vendor blog). — [Baat.ai](https://baat.ai/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-india)

### Inferences
- Billing model for a doctor-replying bot (patients always message first; bot replies in-window with free-form text): cost to Meta = ₹0 for the first 1,000 replies per number per month, then ₹0.115 per delivered reply (India recipients). A solo practice with, say, 3,000 bot replies/month would pay roughly ₹230/month (2,000 × ₹0.115) plus GST and any BSP markup. Outbound template nudges (appointment reminders = utility ₹0.115; health-camp promos = marketing ₹0.8631) are extra and require pre-approved templates and opt-in.
- Because the free tier is per business phone number, a clinic running several doctors' numbers gets 1,000 free replies per number.
- A "patient asks → bot answers" interaction that starts from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad is fully free for up to 7 days (FEP), but this is irrelevant for organic patient contact.
- If the clinic uses Meta Business Agent instead of its own LLM, each reply costs ~4–5¢ (~₹3.5–4.5) regardless of free tiers — more expensive per message than a self-built agent on a cheap model, but with no separate LLM bill.

### Gaps
- Meta's "AI Providers pricing policy (effective February 16, 2026, updated May 12, 2026)" is referenced on the pricing page but its text could not be retrieved (linked page returned 404 under the URL tried). It governs how AI Providers (not ordinary businesses) are charged for service messages.
- The exact July 2025 India rates (as opposed to Oct 2026 rates) were only seen in a third-party support article (₹0.86 marketing / ₹0.13 utility) and are not confirmed from Meta's archive.

## Key Question 3 — BSP / provider costs (platform fees and per-message markups)

### Takeaway
BSPs charge in two layers: a monthly platform fee (₹999–₹5,799/month for Indian SMB tools; €49–€99 per number for 360dialog; $0 for Twilio/Gupshup/Bird pay-as-you-go) and a per-message markup over Meta's rate (Twilio $0.005 per message in or out; Gupshup $0.001; Bird $0.005; Interakt/AiSensy embed a markup of roughly ₹0.01–0.03 on utility/service and ₹0.10–0.23 on marketing; 360dialog "zero message markup"). For a low-volume clinic the platform fee dominates the bill, not Meta's per-message charges.

### Inferences
- Markup arithmetic (Oct 2026, India): Interakt Growth Service ₹0.129 vs Meta ₹0.115 = ~₹0.014 (12%) markup; AiSensy ₹0.145 vs ₹0.115 = ₹0.03 (26%); AiSensy marketing ₹1.09 vs ₹0.8631 = ₹0.23. (Some of these may include GST; the pages do not say.)
- Cheapest legitimate stack for a developer-built agent in India: direct Meta Cloud API (no platform fee, Meta rates only) or 360dialog/Gupshup-style pass-through; the BSP inboxes (Wati/AiSensy/Interakt) make sense only if the clinic wants a human-agent UI.

## Key Question 4 — Meta policy on healthcare, HIPAA, and AI chatbots

### Takeaway
Meta's Business Platform contract says in terms that Meta "is not a Business Associate" and "the WhatsApp Business Platform is not HIPAA compliant"; the Business Messaging Policy forbids using WhatsApp "for telemedicine or to send or request any health related information" where local law requires heightened-security systems, and lists "Medical and healthcare products" and "Drugs" as restricted verticals. The Jan 2026 "AI Providers" clause (s.4.7) bans companies whose primary product is a general-purpose assistant; a clinic's own customer-service bot is "incidental or ancillary" and remains allowed, but WhatsApp Platform data may not be used to train general AI models.

### Cited Findings
- Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform, s.4.2 Prohibited Data (live 20 Oct 2026): "With regard to health information, you acknowledge that Meta is not a Business Associate or Subcontractor (as those terms are defined in the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act ('HIPAA')) and that the WhatsApp Business Platform is not HIPAA compliant. Meta has no liability for Prohibited Information… We make no representations or warranties that the WhatsApp Business Platform meets the needs of businesses regulated by laws and regulations with heightened confidentiality requirements for data, such as healthcare, financial, or legal services." — [Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)
- Same Terms, s.4.7 AI Providers: "Providers and developers of artificial intelligence or machine learning technologies, including but not limited to large language models, generative artificial intelligence platforms, general-purpose artificial intelligence assistants, or similar technologies as determined by Meta in its sole discretion ('AI Providers'), are strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Platform, whether directly or indirectly, for the purposes of providing, delivering, offering, selling, or otherwise making available such technologies when such technologies are the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available for use, as determined by Meta in its sole discretion… Notwithstanding the foregoing, you may retain an AI Provider as your Solution Provider. In such cases, you may not directly or indirectly allow WhatsApp Business Platform Data… to be used to create, develop, train, or improve any machine learning or artificial intelligence systems… provided that you may use WhatsApp Business Platform Data to fine-tune an AI Model that is for your exclusive use." — [Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)
- Effective dates: new API users from 15 Oct 2025; all existing users from 15 Jan 2026; named casualties ChatGPT and Perplexity on WhatsApp; OpenAI confirmed ChatGPT would stop on WhatsApp after 15 Jan 2026. Allowed: "Structured bots for support, bookings, order tracking, notifications and sales"; "the chatbot's role must be ancillary to a legitimate business service, not the centerpiece of the interaction." — [respond.io: WhatsApp's 2026 AI Policy Explained (3 Nov 2025)](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-policy-2026); [Business Standard, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meta-bans-ai-chatbots-from-whatsapp-business-api-chatgpt-to-shut-down-jan-2026-125102200335_1.html)

- Meta's pricing page (Sep 30 2026) confirms ordinary businesses may still have service messages "powered by a 3rd-party AI solution" — i.e., third-party AI replying on a business's own number is a recognised, billable category, not a banned one. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)

- Meta Cloud API security posture: SOC2/SOC3, GDPR/LGPD compliance statements, local data-storage option, "currently pursuing ISO 27001". — [Meta: On-Premises API Sunset](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/on-premises/sunset)

### Inferences
- US: a HIPAA covered entity cannot lawfully route PHI through the Business Platform because Meta refuses BA status; the bot would be a business-associate workflow without a BAA. The practical US pattern is patient-initiated contact with minimum-necessary replies and documented patient preference, or an appointment-logistics-only bot that never handles clinical content.
- India: the Business Messaging Policy clause is conditional ("if applicable regulations prohibit…"). India's Telemedicine Practice Guidelines explicitly name WhatsApp as a permitted channel (KQ5), so the clause does not by itself bar Indian telemedicine over WhatsApp; the DPDP Act then governs the data.
- A clinic-owned triage/FAQ/appointment bot is on the allowed side of s.4.7 (ancillary to the clinic's service). Risk flags: (i) if the bot answers arbitrary health questions for the public it starts to look like a "general-purpose assistant"; (ii) Meta determines "primary vs incidental" in its "sole discretion"; (iii) patient chats may not be used to train shared AI models (fine-tuning for the clinic's exclusive use is permitted).

### Gaps
- Meta's dedicated AI-Providers pricing policy and any 2026 enforcement statistics were not retrievable.
- No Meta statement specific to healthcare chatbots (beyond the general policy clause) was found.

## Key Question 5 — India regulatory context (Telemedicine Guidelines 2020, NMC rules, DPDP Act/Rules, ABDM, liability, AI guidance)

### Takeaway
Indian law allows text/WhatsApp telemedicine by a Registered Medical Practitioner, but the 2020 Telemedicine Practice Guidelines state flatly that AI/ML platforms "are not allowed to counsel the patients or prescribe any medicines"; AI may only "assist and support" the RMP, who must "directly deliver" counselling and prescriptions. The DPDP Act 2023 (Rules notified 13 Nov 2025; core obligations from May 2027) treats health data as ordinary personal data under a consent-first regime with 72-hour breach reporting to the Board and penalties up to ₹250 crore; the doctor/clinic is the Data Fiduciary and the bot vendor a Data Processor. ICMR's 2023 AI guidelines require human-in-the-loop supervision and pre-allocated liability. Doctors are "service" providers under consumer law (IMA v. V.P. Shantha), so a wrong bot answer is a deficiency-of-service exposure for the doctor, not the bot.

### Cited Findings

Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, 25 March 2020 (Board of Governors in supersession of MCI; appended to the IMC Professional Conduct Regulations 2002)
- Permitted modes: "Primarily there are 3 modes: Video, Audio or Text (chat, images, messaging, email, fax etc.)"; chat platforms listed explicitly include "WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger etc." — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (PDF)](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf) (original host: https://www.mohfw.gov.in/pdf/Telemedicine.pdf)
- §5.4: "Technology platforms based on Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning are not allowed to counsel the patients or prescribe any medicines to a patient. Only a RMP is entitled to counsel or prescribe and has to directly communicate with the patient in this regard. While new technologies such as Artificial Intelligence, Internet of Things, advanced data science-based decision support systems etc. could assist and support a RMP on patient evaluation, diagnosis or management, the final prescription or counseling has to be directly delivered by the RMP." §5.7: a violating platform can be "blacklisted, and no RMP may then use that platform". §5.1–5.2: platforms must ensure consumers consult registered RMPs and display each RMP's "name, qualification and registration number, contact details". — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §5](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Identity: §3.2.1 "Telemedicine consultation should not be anonymous: both patient and the RMP need to know each other's identity"; §3.2.2 RMP "should verify and confirm patient's identity by name, age, address, email ID, phone number, registered ID"; §3.2.3 minors only with an identified adult; §3.2.5 "Every RMP shall display the registration number… on prescriptions, website, electronic communication (WhatsApp/ email etc.) and receipts". — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §3.2](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Consent: §3.4.1 "If the patient initiates the telemedicine consultation, then the consent is implied"; §3.4.2 explicit consent is needed if "A Health worker, RMP or a Caregiver initiates"; §3.4.3 explicit consent "can be recorded in any form… email, text or audio/video message" and "The RMP must record this in his patient records." — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §3.4](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Prescribing: medicines split into List O (OTC-type, any mode), List A (first consult only by video, refills on follow-up), List B (follow-up add-ons) and a Prohibited List ("Schedule X of Drug and Cosmetic Act and Rules or any Narcotic and Psychotropic substance"); §3.6.4.2 RMP "shall provide photo, scan, digital copy of a signed prescription or e-Prescription to the patient via email or any messaging platform"; sending directly to a pharmacy needs "explicit consent of the patient". — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §3.6](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Privacy: §3.7.1.2 RMPs must abide by the 2002 Ethics Regulations "and with the relevant provisions of the IT Act, Data protection and privacy laws"; §3.7.1.3 RMPs "will not be held responsible for breach of confidentiality if there is a reasonable evidence to believe that patient's privacy… has been compromised by a technology breach or by a person other than RMP. The RMPs should ensure that reasonable degree of care undertaken during hiring such services." — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §3.7.1](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Health-worker route: a "Health Worker" (nurse, allied health professional, mid-level provider, ANM "or any other health worker designated by an appropriate authority") may facilitate a consult with an RMP; the guidelines do not create any role for non-human intermediaries. — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines §4.2](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)
- Law-firm review (Argus Partners, May 2020) confirms the guidelines were issued "under the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956" and cover "issues of liability and negligence… informed consent… privacy and security of the patient records". — [Mondaq: Telemedicine Guidelines – A Review](https://www.mondaq.com/india/healthcare/928698/telemedicine-guidelines-a-review)

DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025
- Act text: s.4 processing only with consent or for "certain legitimate uses"; s.6(1) consent "shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous"; the Act's own illustration uses a telemedicine app: consent for contact-list access is not valid because "phone contact list is not necessary for making available telemedicine services"; s.6(4) withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent; s.7(f)/(g) permit processing without consent "for responding to a medical emergency involving a threat to the life or immediate threat to the health of the Data Principal" and for treatment "during an epidemic, outbreak of disease, or any other threat to public health"; s.8(5) "reasonable security safeguards"; s.8(6) breach intimation to the Board "and each affected Data Principal"; s.8(7) erasure on withdrawal/purpose exhaustion; s.9 verifiable parental consent for children (<18); s.10 Significant Data Fiduciary designation considers "the volume and sensitivity of personal data processed". The Act contains no separate "sensitive personal data" class — "personal data" is simply "any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data" (s.2(t)); Data Fiduciary / Data Processor defined at s.2(i)/(k). — [DPDP Act 2023 (MeitY PDF)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)
- Penalties (Schedule, s.33): up to ₹250 crore (security safeguards), ₹200 crore (breach notification), ₹200 crore (children), ₹150 crore (SDF obligations), ₹10,000 (Data Principal duties), ₹50 crore (any other breach). — [DPDP Act 2023 Schedule](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf); summarised in [EY India DPDP Act & Rules POV (Nov 2025)](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/pdf/2025/11/ey-india-dpdp-act-2023-and-rules-2025-pov.pdf)
- Rules notified: "official notification of the final DPDP Rules by MeitY on 13 November 2025" (EY); BDO/Livemint report "14 November". Phasing: Data Protection Board provisions immediate; "Rule 4 [Consent Manager] shall come into force one year after the date of publication" (Nov 2026); remaining obligations 18 months out (13/14 May 2027). — [EY POV](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/pdf/2025/11/ey-india-dpdp-act-2023-and-rules-2025-pov.pdf); [BDO India, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties); [StartupTalky, 24 Sep 2026](https://startuptalky.com/dpdp-act-timeline-phases-2026-2027-explained/) (gives 14 Nov 2025 / 13 Nov 2026 / 14 May 2027)
- Rules content (EY summary): Rule 3 notice content in "clear and plain language"; Rule 6 reasonable security safeguards (encryption, access control, logs); Rule 7 breach: notify affected Data Principals and the Board "without any delay" and "Submit a report with updated and detailed information related to the breach to the Board within 72 hours"; Rule 8 retention/erasure with 48-hour pre-erasure notice and one-year log retention (Third Schedule timelines apply to specified classes of fiduciary); Rule 9 publish contact of a person who answers data questions; Rules 10–11 verifiable consent for children/persons with disability; Rule 12 exemptions — Fourth Schedule exempts e.g. healthcare/education providers from parts of the children's-data conditions "where children's data is processed solely for health-protection purposes"; Rule 13 SDF duties; Rule 15 cross-border transfer subject to government restrictions. — [EY POV](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/pdf/2025/11/ey-india-dpdp-act-2023-and-rules-2025-pov.pdf); [BDO India](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties)
- Pre-DPDP regime still cited by the Telemedicine Guidelines: the IT Act 2000 and the IT (Reasonable Security Practices… Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules 2011 "primarily govern the practice of medicine and information technology" (as of 2020). — [Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, Background](https://esanjeevani.mohfw.gov.in/assets/guidelines/Telemedicine_Practice_Guidelines.pdf)

ICMR Ethical Guidelines for Application of AI in Biomedical Research and Healthcare (2023)
- Autonomy: "Humans should have complete control of the AI based healthcare system and medical decision-making. The AI technology should not interfere with patient autonomy under any circumstances"; "patients should be fully informed about the use of AI technologies benefits and associated… risks. Patients must have complete autonomy to choose or reject AI technologies." — [ICMR 2023 guidelines (PDF)](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Ethical_Guidelines_AI_Healthcare_2023.pdf)
- Accountability §1.5: "unlike other fields of AI technologies where unsupervised deployment is common, AI for health should always be appropriately supervised"; HITL "places human beings in a supervisory role"; "the responsibility of optimal utilization of the technology is on the health professional using AI-based solutions"; "the legal responsibility of its usage needs to be defined before adopting it into clinical or public use"; "If the malfunction is primarily due to flaws in functionality, then the designer, developer, or manufacturer may be held responsible. If the harm is caused due to defective implementation… then the end-user or organization may be held accountable." AI tools "must undergo regular internal and external audits… These audit reports must be made publicly available." — [ICMR 2023](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Ethical_Guidelines_AI_Healthcare_2023.pdf)
- The guidelines name chatbots as a legitimate use case in mental health ("initial assistance for people who are not ready for professional… care"). — [ICMR 2023](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Ethical_Guidelines_AI_Healthcare_2023.pdf)

MeitY AI advisories (2024)
- The 1 March 2024 advisory asked platforms to seek government permission before deploying "under-tested/unreliable" AI; the revised 15 March 2024 advisory dropped the permission requirement and instead advises firms "to label under-tested and unreliable AI models to inform users of their potential fallibility", to use "consent popups" about AI-output unreliability, to avoid bias/unlawful content, and to label synthetic media; neither advisory was published online and MeitY said it "wasn't legally binding". — [TechCrunch, 15 Mar 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/15/india-drops-plan-to-require-approval-for-ai-model-launches/)

Consumer protection / negligence
- Supreme Court, Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha (13 Nov 1995): medical practitioners and hospitals rendering service for consideration are "service" under the Consumer Protection Act, so patients are "consumers" who can sue for deficiency in service. — [Indian Kanoon: IMA v. V.P. Shantha](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/723973/)

### Inferences
- A bot that merely triages, collects history, books appointments, sends the RMP's signed prescription, or answers administrative questions sits inside the guidelines; a bot that itself tells a patient what to take or what their symptoms mean is "counselling/prescribing" and is prohibited under §5.4 regardless of disclaimers. The RMP (not the vendor) faces NMC disciplinary action, and the platform can be blacklisted.
- Because patient-initiated chat = implied consent (TPG §3.4.1), the bot may respond to inbound messages, but any bot-initiated outreach (reminders, follow-ups) needs explicit, recorded consent — which dovetails with Meta's opt-in rule (KQ7).
- Under DPDP, the doctor/clinic is the Data Fiduciary; the bot vendor, BSP and LLM provider are Data Processors, requiring contracts, security safeguards, 72-hour breach reporting, erasure flows and a published grievance contact. Penalty exposure (up to ₹250 crore) is theoretical for a solo clinic but the Board can fine proportionately.
- Although DPDP lacks a "sensitive" class, the SPDI Rules 2011 (which classify medical records as sensitive) remain in force until the DPDP phases supersede the IT Act provisions (2027), so both regimes matter during the build window.
- Liability chain for a wrong bot answer: patient → doctor/clinic (consumer deficiency / negligence) → contractual recourse against the bot vendor; ICMR expects this allocation to be written down before deployment.

## Key Question 6 — US/EU context: HIPAA texting, GDPR/AI Act classification, MDR software thresholds

### Takeaway
US: HIPAA makes any messaging vendor that transmits PHI on a provider's behalf a business associate requiring a BAA; Meta disclaims that role, so a US practice cannot put PHI through WhatsApp compliantly. EU: health data is a GDPR Art. 9 special category; a triage chatbot that influences diagnosis/treatment is very likely a medical device (MDR Rule 11, class IIa+) and therefore automatically a high-risk AI system (AI Act Art. 6(1)/Annex I, deadline 2 Aug 2028); an emergency-triage tool is high-risk via Annex III(5)(d) (deadline 2 Dec 2027 after the Digital Omnibus); appointment-management tools are expressly excluded; all patient-facing chatbots owe Art. 50 "you are talking to an AI" transparency from 2 Aug 2026.

### Cited Findings
- HIPAA BA rule: see HHS cloud guidance quoted in KQ4 — a vendor that creates/receives/maintains/transmits ePHI "is a business associate under HIPAA… even if the CSP processes or stores only encrypted ePHI and lacks an encryption key"; a BAA is mandatory. — [HHS: Guidance on HIPAA & Cloud Computing](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html)
- Meta: "Meta is not a Business Associate… the WhatsApp Business Platform is not HIPAA compliant." — [Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform s.4.2](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)
- GDPR Art. 9(1): processing of "data concerning health" is prohibited unless an exception applies, e.g. Art. 9(2)(h) "preventive or occupational medicine… medical diagnosis, the provision of health or social care or treatment" under professional secrecy, or explicit consent (Art. 9(2)(a)). — [GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)
- AI Act Annex III(5)(d) lists as high-risk "AI systems intended to evaluate and classify emergency calls… or to establish priority in the dispatching of, emergency first response services… as well as of emergency healthcare patient triage systems." — [EU AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Timeline (site last updated 31 Aug 2026): 2 Aug 2026 "The remainder of the AI Act starts to apply, unless specified otherwise"; 2 Dec 2027 "Chapter III, Sections 1, 2, and 3… start applying as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article 6(2) and Annex III"; Annex I (product-embedded) high-risk systems have until 2 Aug 2028. — [EU AI Act implementation timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/); [Praxikon/aiactblog, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/medical-ai-ai-act-and-mdr-combined-conformity) ("This date is fixed in Regulation (EU) 2026/1744"; "the transparency obligations of Article 50, for instance for a patient-facing chatbot, have applied since 2 August 2026 and have not been postponed")
- MDR route: "classification Rule 11 in Annex VIII of the Medical Device Regulation places software that provides information for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions in class IIa or higher in nearly all cases. Only software with no influence on clinical decisions stays in class I." Class IIa+ → notified body → "your AI system is high-risk, without any separate risk assessment under the AI Act" (Art. 6(1)); one combined conformity assessment with the same notified body. — [Praxikon/aiactblog](https://www.aiactblog.nl/en/posts/medical-ai-ai-act-and-mdr-combined-conformity)
- Commission draft Art. 6(5) guidelines (published for consultation to 23 Jul 2026, per Osborne Clarke 18 Jun 2026): MDR/IVDR products needing third-party conformity assessment are high-risk; "Even for Class I medical device software, where self-certification is theoretically available, it cannot be used to argue out of high-risk AI status"; "Incorporating a clinician review step into deployment does not affect high-risk classification… human oversight is a compliance prerequisite once a system is classified as high risk, not a route around that classification"; tools inferring health status from physiological/biometric signals are high-risk under biometric categorisation. Search-result summary of the same article (not re-verified in full text): an ED prioritisation tool and "a mental health crisis triage chatbot that assesses urgency" are cited as high-risk, while "tools used to manage medical appointments, patient identification systems and transcription tools for emergency calls are explicitly excluded". — [Osborne Clarke, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/eu-draft-guidelines-sharpen-high-risk-ai-classification-medtech-and-digital-health)
- Article 50 transparency: providers must ensure people are informed they are interacting with an AI system unless obvious. — [EU AI Act Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)

### Inferences
- The "becomes a regulated medical device" threshold is intended purpose: a bot that routes messages, books slots, sends reminders or returns the doctor's own words is communication/admin software (not MDR, not Annex III); the moment it assesses symptoms to advise urgency or treatment for an individual, MDR Rule 11 (class IIa+) and AI Act high-risk duties attach in the EU. A "not medical advice" banner does not change intended purpose.
- US practices should treat WhatsApp as unavailable for PHI unless the workflow is limited to non-PHI logistics; EU practices can use the Cloud API (Meta asserts GDPR compliance and offers local storage) under Art. 9(2)(h)/consent with a DPA, but the bot's clinical scope drives MDR/AI-Act obligations.

### Gaps
- HHS's FAQ on texting/e-mailing patients (FAQ 570 / 3014) could not be fetched (HHS blocked non-browser access / page moved); the patient-initiated, minimum-necessary position is reported only in vendor summaries.
- MDCG 2019-11 (rev. 1, 2025) was not read directly; its tests are described via secondary analyses.
- Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus) was not read directly; dates are from the AI Act explorer site and Praxikon.

## Key Question 7 — Practical constraints: messaging limits, quality rating, opt-in, voice notes, images/documents, groups

### Takeaway
Inbound replies are unconstrained by messaging limits (limits only cap outbound template reach outside the 24-hour window: 250 → 2,000 → 10,000 → 100,000 → unlimited unique users per rolling 24 h). Quality rating (green/yellow/red, driven by blocks/reports over 7 days) gates scaling. Opt-in is mandatory before business-initiated messaging. The Cloud API receives voice notes (audio/ogg Opus, plus AAC/AMR/MP3/M4A, 16 MB), images (JPEG/PNG, 5 MB) and documents incl. PDF (100 MB) via webhooks with 7-day media download windows — so prescriptions, lab reports and voice notes are all usable. Groups are now API-accessible (Groups API, 8 participants, Official Business Accounts only, not for Business-App/coexistence numbers).

### Cited Findings
- Messaging limits (page updated 21 May 2026): "Messaging limits are the maximum number of unique WhatsApp user phone numbers your business can deliver messages to, outside of a customer service window, within a moving 24-hour period." Set "at the business portfolio level"; new portfolios start at 250; 2,000 after a scaling path (business verification, partner verification, or 2,000 delivered high-quality template messages in 30 days); then 10,000 / 100,000 / unlimited automatically when "you are sending high-quality messages" and "In the last 7 days, your business has utilized at least half of your current messaging limit" (increase within 6 hours). — [Meta: Messaging Limits](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/messaging-limits)
- Quality rating: "based on the recent messages that your customers have received over the past seven days… determined by feedback from your customers, such as the reason why they blocked your number"; Green/Yellow/Red; block reasons "No longer needed, Didn't sign up, Spam, Offensive messages, No reason"; "The phone number statuses of Flagged and Restricted have been discontinued as of 7 October 2025." — [Meta Business Help: quality rating](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/896873687365001)
- Opt-in (page updated 16 Jun 2026): "Businesses are required to obtain opt-in before messaging people on WhatsApp… As per the November 2024 WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy update… opt-in permission… can be general and not specifically for WhatsApp"; must state the business name and that the person is opting in; methods include SMS, website, IVR, "In person or on paper"; "Our systems will rate limit businesses if the business's quality is low for a sustained period of time." — [Meta: Get opt-in for WhatsApp](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/overview/getting-opt-in)
- Media (page updated 16 Jun 2026): audio types AAC, AMR, MP3, MP4 audio (.m4a), OGG ("audio/ogg (OPUS codecs only; base audio/ogg not supported; mono input only)"), all 16 MB; documents (.txt/.xls/.xlsx/.doc/.docx/.ppt/.pptx/.pdf) 100 MB; images JPEG/PNG 5 MB ("8-bit, RGB or RGBA"); video 3GPP/MP4 16 MB (H.264 + AAC); "Media IDs in webhooks expire after 7 days"; "Media URLs expire after 5 minutes"; inbound files over 100 MB trigger error 131052; uploaded media "are encrypted and persist for 30 days". — [Meta: Media (Cloud API)](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/reference/media)
- Groups API (page updated 15 Oct 2026): "now open to all businesses with an Official Business Account (OBA)"; "Max group participants: 8"; "Max groups you can create: 10,000 per business number"; "Max Cloud API businesses per group: 1"; supported: text, media, text/media templates; not supported: calling, disappearing, view-once, auth, commerce, interactive messages; "Groups are not available for WhatsApp Business app phone numbers and phone numbers onboarded to Multi-solution Conversations"; per-message pricing (webhook type free_group_customer_service exists for free group service messages). — [Meta: Groups API](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/groups)
- Calling: user-initiated calls also open the 24-hour window; the Business Calling API has separate pricing. — [Meta Pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)
- Coexistence throughput and app-feature losses: see KQ1 (Seasalt: 20 msg/s under coexistence; 360dialog: 80 msg/s standard; disappearing/view-once unavailable after onboarding).
- Unofficial libraries can read group chats and all media (whatsapp-web.js feature table), which is the capability gap that tempts developers toward them. — [whatsapp-web.js README](https://github.com/pedroslopez/whatsapp-web.js)

### Inferences
- A reply-only clinic bot never hits messaging limits; limits matter only if the clinic also sends reminders/campaigns. Low quality rating (patients blocking the number) would throttle those outbound sends and could jeopardise coexistence eligibility, which Meta ties to "messaging quality".
- Voice-note handling is fully feasible: webhook → download .ogg (Opus) within 7 days → speech-to-text → LLM. Prescription photos (JPEG/PNG ≤5 MB) and PDF reports (≤100 MB) can be fetched the same way; the bot must store them under DPDP safeguards rather than leaving them in WhatsApp media URLs that expire in 5 minutes.
